## 1. Purpose and Scope

This procedure sets out when a CAPA is required, how it is investigated using the 8D method, how due dates are assigned, how status is tracked and how effectiveness is verified. It applies to all plants and to supplier corrective actions managed through a SCAR.

## 2. When a CAPA Is Required

A CAPA record must be opened when any of the following occurs:

1. Three or more Major NCRs (QP-8.7) for the same defect code on the same line within 30 days.
2. A single Major NCR with a safety, regulatory or customer-shipped impact.
3. A Customer Complaint NCR that is confirmed valid.
4. A supplier's incoming PPM exceeds the limit in its supplier quality agreement for a month, or a SCAR is issued (the SCAR is tracked as a CAPA).
5. A Cpk for a critical characteristic falls below 1.33 on two consecutive capability studies.
6. An audit finding (internal, customer or certification body) at the major level.
7. A Quality Management Review action requires systemic correction.

CAPA numbers use the format CAPA-YYYY-NNN and are assigned in the CAPA register by the plant quality engineer or supplier quality engineer.

## 3. The 8D Method

All CAPAs rated Major follow the eight disciplines. The 8D report has its own record number (for example 8D-WRO-2026-009).

| Step | Name | Required output |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | Form the team | Cross-functional team, leader and sponsor named |
| D2 | Describe the problem | Is / is-not analysis, quantified with defect data |
| D3 | Interim containment | Actions that protect the customer; verified with data |
| D4 | Root cause | Occurrence and escape root causes verified by test; 5-Why or fishbone |
| D5 | Choose permanent corrective actions | Actions with owners and dates; verified to resolve the root cause |
| D6 | Implement and validate | Actions implemented; interim containment removed only after evidence |
| D7 | Prevent recurrence | Update control plans, FMEA, work instructions, training; extend to similar processes |
| D8 | Recognize the team and close | Lessons learned documented; sign-off |

Containment (D3) must be in place within 24 hours of CAPA opening for Major issues. Supplier 8D responses follow the timing in the supplier quality agreement (for Brightline Castings, SQA-BLC-2023: SCAR response in 10 business days, 8D within 30 days).

## 4. Due-Date Rules

The due date is set at opening and approved by the Director of Quality for Major CAPAs.

| Category | Criteria | Due |
|---|---|---|
| Expedited | Safety or regulatory risk, customer shipment affected | 30 days |
| Standard Major | In-plant process issue, root cause expected to be local | 45 days |
| Complex | Supplier, equipment-condition or multi-plant issues requiring a long lead time or engineering change | 60 days |

Examples: CAPA-2026-101 (supplier SCAR, 60 days), CAPA-2026-102 (in-plant process, 45 days) and CAPA-2026-103 (equipment condition, 60 days).

Due dates may be extended once, by up to 30 days, with written justification approved by the Director of Quality. A second extension requires the VP Operations. An extension is recorded in the CAPA register as EXT-n with the new date; the original due date is retained.

## 5. Status Definitions

| Status | Definition |
|---|---|
| Open | CAPA record created; investigation not yet started or no containment verified |
| In Progress | Team formed, containment in place, investigation or corrective action under way and on schedule or under an approved extension |
| Overdue | The due date has passed, no approved extension is on file and the CAPA is not Closed. Set automatically by the register |
| Closed | All actions complete and effectiveness verified (Section 6) |

Note that a CAPA can be Open and past its due date. The register reports these as "Open (overdue)" for visibility, as for CAPA-2026-103. Overdue CAPAs are reviewed weekly by the plant manager and reported in the monthly Quality Management Review.

## 6. Effectiveness Verification

A CAPA cannot be closed on the strength of completed actions alone. The CAPA owner defines an effectiveness check at D5 and the quality engineer performs it after implementation (D6).

1. The monitoring window is at least 90 days or 3 production lots, whichever is longer. For supplier CAPAs, it is 3 consecutive months.
2. Success criteria are quantitative and set in advance, for example: in-process defect rate returns to baseline (about 1.2% to 1.5% for crimp height defects), supplier PPM at or below the contractual limit, Cpk at or above 1.33.
3. If criteria are not met, the CAPA is reopened at D4.
4. Verification evidence (data extract, control chart, audit record) is attached to the record.

## 7. Roles

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| CAPA owner | Leads the 8D, maintains the register entry, meets dates |
| Sponsor | Plant manager or director; removes obstacles and approves resources |
| Quality engineer | Verifies containment and effectiveness |
| Director of Quality | Approves due dates and extensions; chairs the review |

## 8. Records and Metrics

The Director of Quality reports the following monthly: number of open CAPAs by plant, number overdue, average days to close, and the percentage closed on time. The target is at least 90% on-time closure. A CAPA that is more than 30 days overdue is escalated to the VP Operations.
